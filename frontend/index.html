The problem

Maps show the shortest way. They do not show the missing ramp.

For wheelchair users, older people and anyone recovering from an injury, one set of stairs can end a journey. Navigation apps rank routes by distance and time, and Indian cities have almost no structured record of these barriers.

Today
  • Barriers are found by walking into them
  • No shared record of what is broken or blocked
  • Authorities cannot see which problem affects the most people
With AccessPulse
  • Barriers are pinned on a map by the people who meet them
  • Each report shows its status: pending, verified, rejected or resolved
  • Administrators export the full record as a CSV file

How it works

From a pin on the map to a reviewed record

  1. Step 1

    Pin the barrier

    Choose one of six barrier types, describe what you saw and mark the location.

  2. Step 2

    An administrator reviews it

    A signed in administrator verifies or rejects the report. Verified means reviewed, not inspected by a professional.

  3. Step 3

    See it on the dashboard

    The map, the status counts and the barrier type chart update with the new record. Fixed barriers are marked resolved.

Every pin is a report, never a promise. AccessPulse shows what the community saw, who reviewed it, and what nobody has checked yet.

The score uses severity, nearby reports, review status and waiting time. The severity weights are our own judgement, not an official standard.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is AccessPulse?

A web application where people report physical accessibility barriers on a map. An administrator reviews each report, and a dashboard summarises the results.

Does it give me an accessible route?

No. Markers show submitted reports, not certified accessible routes. A route that passes no marker is not proof that it is safe.

What does verified mean?

An administrator reviewed the report and accepted it. It is not a legal compliance check or a professional inspection.

Who can submit a report?

Anyone. You do not need an account. New reports begin as pending until an administrator reviews them.

What happens when a barrier is fixed?

An administrator marks it as resolved. The change is saved with who made it and when, and the report shows as resolved on the map. If the barrier comes back, it can be reopened.

Can I add a photo?

Yes, a photo is optional. The server saves a clean copy of each JPG or PNG, up to 5 MB, which removes location and camera data. Faces and number plates are not blurred yet, so please avoid photographing people.

Where is the data stored?

In the prototype, reports are saved in a local SQLite database on the machine running the application. It is a demo setup, not shared production storage.

Can I use it today?

The prototype runs locally and has not been published for public use. This page shows how it works with screenshots.

Privacy

The prototype stores each report's title, description, coordinates, source and time, and a photo if one is added. Do not enter personal details in a report. Map tiles load from Esri and OpenStreetMap, and place search uses OpenStreetMap.

Terms

AccessPulse is a student demonstration. Reports are community submissions reviewed by an administrator. They are not a certified accessibility audit and do not guarantee that any route is safe or accessible.
